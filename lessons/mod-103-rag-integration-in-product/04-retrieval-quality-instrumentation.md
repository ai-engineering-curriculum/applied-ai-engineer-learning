# Retrieval Quality Instrumentation at the Product Altitude

## Why measure on the surface, not the retriever

The retriever team has its own metrics. They matter — MRR, NDCG, offline recall against an internal benchmark, the reranker's win rate against a baseline. Those numbers correlate with product outcomes; they do not equal them.

At the product altitude, the questions are different:

- On real user queries (not benchmark queries), does the retrieval bring back a passage that would let a competent generator answer correctly?
- When it does, does the generator actually use it? Are the emitted citations faithful to the passages they cite?
- When it doesn't, does the surface take the "no good evidence" branch instead of hallucinating?
- Across surfaces sharing the same retriever, which surface is being underserved right now?

You can win an offline recall bake-off and still ship a surface that's wrong 30 % of the time — a mismatch between the eval set and live queries, a prompt that doesn't consume the passages well, a citation UX that surfaces broken links. Product-altitude metrics catch those.

This chapter is the applied-AI engineer's instrumentation stack: a labelled gold set for offline recall@k, a live hit-rate metric, sampled online judges for faithfulness and answer-relevancy, and a per-surface RAG-triad readout that ties them together.

## The gold set

### What a gold set is (and isn't)

A gold set is a labelled corpus of realistic queries — with, for each query, the passages a good retriever should surface. It is:

- **Owned by the applied-AI engineer** for the queries (they know the product);
- **Owned by the `rag-engineer` peer track** for the passage labels (they know the corpus);
- **Version-controlled** in a repo both teams commit to;
- **Sized deliberately** — 100–500 queries per surface is enough to move recall@k off noise; 5000 queries is diminishing returns and a curation tax nobody pays.

It is *not*:

- A synthetic set generated by an LLM against the corpus. Synthetic sets over-represent what LLMs think users would ask and under-represent the ambiguity, typos, and product-specific vocabulary of real queries. Use synthetics as a seed if you must, but promote them into the gold set only after a human confirms they resemble live traffic.
- The retriever team's benchmark. That benchmark evaluates the retriever against the corpus; the gold set evaluates the *surface* against the retriever's output for the product's actual query distribution.
- Static forever. The gold set has a lifecycle — see "curation" below.

### The query mix

A gold set that only contains "clean, well-formed questions the corpus obviously answers" tells you nothing about live behaviour. Aim, per surface, for a stratified mix:

- **Head queries** — the top 20–50 patterns that dominate real traffic. Weighted heavily.
- **Torso queries** — mid-frequency patterns with real product intent.
- **Long-tail** — rare-but-important queries; regulatory, compliance, corner cases.
- **Ambiguous** — queries that plausibly resolve to more than one document. The label lists all acceptable passages.
- **Impossible** — queries the corpus genuinely cannot answer. The correct behaviour is "no good evidence" — see the coverage metric below.
- **Adversarial** — queries designed to trigger a known failure mode (prompt injection in the query, near-neighbours to sensitive documents, cross-tenant IDs).

Track the mix ratios in the gold set metadata. When the eval score moves, being able to decompose "which stratum drove it" is the difference between "the surface got better" and "we got lucky on head queries."

### The labels

For each query, record:

- `must_contain_any`: a list of passage IDs, any one of which is sufficient (this is your recall@k oracle).
- `must_contain_all` (optional): for multi-hop or multi-source queries where a good answer needs *both* passages.
- `must_not_contain` (optional): passages that would be wrong to cite (superseded policy, cross-tenant leakage sanity check).
- `is_impossible`: `true` for the impossible stratum; the correct pack is empty.
- `expected_answer_shape` (optional): a short human note. Not scored; useful when triaging failures.
- `notes`: why this query is in the set, so the next curator understands it.

### Curation lifecycle

Gold sets rot. New product surfaces expose queries that weren't in the mix. Refresh cadences that used to be safe drift out of scope. Regularly:

- **Rotate**: retire queries that are no longer representative (the head query pattern shifted).
- **Add**: pull weekly from a random sample of live queries, hand-label 5–10 per week.
- **Adversarial refresh**: after each red-team pass, promote any query that broke the surface into the adversarial stratum.
- **Re-label on corpus change**: when the corpus manifest ([chapter 02](02-freshness-coverage-permission-contract.md)) changes non-trivially, re-verify labels against the new content.

Owning the lifecycle is the applied-AI engineer's job. If the gold set is 18 months old and nobody has touched it, the surface has no eval — it has a nostalgia benchmark.

## Recall@k on the gold set

Given the gold set, compute `recall@k` per surface: for each query, does the retriever return at least one passage from `must_contain_any` in its top `k`? Aggregate. Report:

- `recall@1`, `recall@5`, `recall@k_max` for the surface's production `k`.
- Same numbers broken down per query stratum (head / torso / long-tail / ambiguous / impossible / adversarial). The mean across strata hides the interesting story.
- `mrr` (mean reciprocal rank) as a secondary signal — recall counts whether the passage made it in; MRR counts how *near the top* it made it in.

Run it in three places:

- **CI**, on every PR that touches the retrieval client, the query rewriter, the prompt, or the retriever contract. Fail the build on regression beyond a threshold.
- **Nightly**, on production against the current staging retriever. Ensures a change on the retriever team's side that ships without you noticing is caught the next morning.
- **Pre-release**, before every retriever version bump. Both teams sign off on the readout.

A recall@k trend line that looks flat while other product metrics move is your reminder that recall@k is one signal among several, not the whole story.

## Hit rate on live traffic

Recall@k lives on the gold set. **Hit rate** lives on live traffic and answers a different question: given the actual queries production sees, what fraction of them return a non-empty, usable pack?

Definitions matter:

- **Pack returned rate**: fraction of queries where the retriever returned at least one passage. Sanity check; if this dips, the retriever is broken or the query rewriter is producing empty queries.
- **Usable-pack rate**: fraction of queries where at least one returned passage cleared the surface's usability bar — above the retriever's published score threshold for this surface, ACL-visible to the user, source not currently suppressed for freshness.
- **Cited-pack rate**: fraction of queries where the generator actually cited at least one passage. A high pack-returned rate with a low cited rate is the classic "the retriever brought back irrelevant stuff and the model politely declined" pattern.
- **No-good-evidence rate**: fraction of queries where the surface took the [chapter 03](03-citations-and-evidence-ux.md) fallback. This should not be zero (an honest surface refuses sometimes) and should not be dominant (a broken surface refuses always).

Report all four per surface per day and alert on step changes. Live hit-rate movement is often the earliest signal of a retriever regression, days before a user-facing metric drifts.

## Faithfulness and answer-relevancy — the online judges

You cannot label every live answer. You *can* sample a small fraction (1–5 %, calibrated to your traffic) and run an LLM-as-judge on:

- **Faithfulness**: for each cited sentence, does the cited passage actually support the claim? Score per sentence, aggregate per answer.
- **Answer-relevancy**: does the answer address the user's query, or does it drift?
- **Context-relevancy** (a retriever-side signal you consume): were the retrieved passages relevant to the query, independent of what the generator did with them?

The three together form the **RAG triad** — Faithfulness, Answer-Relevancy, Context-Relevancy. Each isolates a different failure mode:

- Low context-relevancy, everything else fine → the retriever brought back the wrong stuff. File with the retriever team.
- High context-relevancy, low faithfulness → the model ignored the passages and hallucinated. Prompt / model / generator issue.
- High context-relevancy, high faithfulness, low answer-relevancy → the passages and citations were right, but the answer drifted from the question. Prompt problem or a query-rewriting bug ([chapter 05](05-feature-side-levers.md)).
- All three low → integration is broken; start at the retriever contract.

### Running the judges

- **Choose a judge model deliberately.** A cheaper model can judge; a stronger judge catches more subtle faithfulness failures. Publish which judge you're using and version it — a judge model swap is an eval regression event that must be labelled as such.
- **Publish the judge rubric.** "Score 1–5, where 5 means every claim is directly supported by a cited passage's text; 3 means…" — publish and freeze the rubric. Reviewers should be able to score by hand against the same rubric.
- **Calibrate the judge against a human-labelled subset.** Score 200 answers by hand; run the judge on the same 200; measure agreement. If the judge disagrees with humans more than 15–20 % of the time, tighten the rubric or upgrade the judge. Re-calibrate whenever you swap models.
- **Report both means and distributions.** A mean faithfulness of 4.3 with a fat tail of 1s is not the same story as a mean of 4.3 with everything between 4 and 5. The tail is where the failures live.

### Two named traps

- **Judge-model overlap.** Using the same model to generate the answer *and* to judge it inflates faithfulness scores — the judge is charitable to the model's own phrasings. Use different model families for generation and judging, or use rule-based checks where the domain permits.
- **Judge drift across releases.** A judge that scored 4.2 last week and 3.8 this week could be a real regression *or* a judge-model behaviour change. Version-stamp the judge and re-run the calibration when it moves.

## RAG triad per surface

Aggregate the four things — pack rate, cited rate, faithfulness, answer-relevancy — plus recall@k, and present a per-surface readout:

| Surface | recall@5 (gold) | pack-returned rate | usable-pack rate | cited-pack rate | no-good-evidence rate | faithfulness (mean / p10) | answer-relevancy (mean / p10) | context-relevancy (mean / p10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| helpdesk-reply-copilot | 0.87 | 0.98 | 0.94 | 0.91 | 0.05 | 4.4 / 3.5 | 4.5 / 3.6 | 4.3 / 3.4 |
| policy-qa | 0.82 | 0.96 | 0.90 | 0.85 | 0.10 | 4.6 / 4.0 | 4.3 / 3.4 | 4.5 / 3.8 |
| sales-collateral-search | 0.74 | 0.94 | 0.81 | 0.75 | 0.15 | 4.1 / 3.0 | 4.2 / 3.1 | 4.0 / 2.9 |

The p10 column is not vanity. A surface where the mean faithfulness is 4.5 but the p10 is 2.0 is a surface where one call in ten is grossly unfaithful. That's a shippable-in-demo, not-shippable-in-production result. Track p10, not just the mean.

Slice the table by:

- Query stratum (head vs. long-tail vs. adversarial).
- Tenant (per-tenant regressions catch content-specific issues before they become bug reports).
- Corpus / source (a single degraded source drags the whole surface's average — call it out).
- Model / prompt version (mod-102 chapter 05's versioning table).
- Retriever version (from the versions readout in [chapter 01](01-consuming-a-retriever-you-do-not-own.md)).

## The citation-quality metrics

The RAG triad answers "is the answer good." Two smaller metrics answer "are the citations doing their job":

- **Citation hallucination rate.** From [chapter 03](03-citations-and-evidence-ux.md)'s parse-time guard: fraction of generations where the model emitted an ID not in the pack. Should be tiny (< 0.1 %); a rising rate is a prompt or model regression.
- **Cited-but-unsupported rate.** From the faithfulness judge: fraction of cited sentences that the cited passage does not actually support. This is the honest hallucination number; it survives even when citation hallucination is at zero.

Both are cheap to compute continuously; add them to the daily readout.

## Tooling — what to reach for, and what not to

You do not need to build all of the above from scratch. Several tools cover parts of it:

- **RAGAS** (`ragas`) — an open library that computes Faithfulness, Answer-Relevancy, Context-Relevancy, and related metrics with configurable judge models. Its rubrics have moved; version-pin.
- **TruLens** — a per-call trace + evaluation framework with feedback functions for the RAG triad, useful for both offline eval and live sampling.
- **DeepEval / promptfoo** — offline test-runners that compose well with the gold set.
- **LangSmith / LangFuse / Weave / Arize Phoenix** — trace + evaluation platforms that will happily run judges on your traffic; verify the judge rubric and versioning story before adopting.
- **BEIR / MTEB** — public retrieval benchmarks. Useful for the retriever team; **not a substitute for your gold set** at the product altitude. Never trade a real product-labelled recall for a leaderboard number.

Don't reach for tools before you have the gold set and the RAG-triad table. The tools are accelerators once you know what you want them to compute; adopted first, they become "we run RAGAS and the number is X" without a defensible story for what the number should be.

## What to do with the readout

A metric that no one owns is a metric no one improves. Wire the readout in:

- **A weekly review** where both teams look at the per-surface table and pick one regression or one persistent low column to address.
- **CI gates** — a merged PR that drops recall@k on the gold set by more than a threshold fails the build.
- **Incident triage** — a live faithfulness dip becomes a page. The runbook says which team owns which triad column so triage doesn't stall on ownership.
- **Rollback signal** — a retriever or prompt version that ships and drops the readout gets rolled back on the same-day.
- **Prioritisation input** — the "which lever next" decisions from [chapter 05](05-feature-side-levers.md) are made on this readout, not on vibes.

## Anti-patterns

1. **Recall@k without a gold set.** A number computed against synthetic queries. Looks scientific; measures nothing about real users.
2. **Faithfulness as a single scalar.** Reporting mean-only hides the tail; report p10 (or p05) alongside.
3. **A judge model that also generated the answer.** Inflates faithfulness by ~10–20 %. Use a different family or a rule-based check where possible.
4. **Live hit-rate as the only production metric.** Hit rate can be high while faithfulness collapses — the retriever's fine, the generator is lying. Read the triad.
5. **A frozen gold set.** Curation is not a launch task; it's a maintenance responsibility. A gold set 12 months out of date is not a gold set.
6. **RAG triad in isolation.** The triad is a diagnostic layer, not the top-line product metric. Tie it to the product KPI in mod-106; the triad is *how* you improve the KPI, not a substitute for it.

## Summary

- Retriever-side metrics correlate with product outcomes; they do not equal them. Instrument at the product altitude.
- The gold set is the load-bearing eval artefact: 100–500 labelled queries per surface, stratified across head / torso / long-tail / ambiguous / impossible / adversarial, curated on a schedule.
- Compute recall@k in CI, nightly, and pre-release. Break down by stratum; the mean hides the story.
- Live hit rate has four flavours — pack returned, usable pack, cited pack, no-good-evidence — and each catches a different failure mode.
- Sample 1–5 % of live traffic through faithfulness / answer-relevancy / context-relevancy judges. Publish the rubric, version the judge, calibrate against humans, watch the tail (p10) as well as the mean.
- The RAG triad table per surface — sliced by stratum, tenant, source, model version, retriever version — is your daily readout. Add citation-hallucination and cited-but-unsupported rates alongside.
- Six anti-patterns collapse the eval: no gold set, mean-only reporting, self-judging, hit-rate-only production monitoring, frozen gold sets, and triad divorced from the product KPI.

Chapter 05 turns this readout into a decision framework for the applied-side levers — query rewriting, classification-gated retrieval, HyDE, step-back, and reranker consumption — so you know which lever to pull when the readout regresses.
